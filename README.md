## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
> Solution outline:

FarmLink Direct is an AI enabled online marketplace that helps farmers, Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs), consumers, retailers, hotels and bulk buyers connect directly.
Farmers will be able to register an account, add their agricultural products, how many they have and what they want to charge. Consumers and bulk buyers will be able to see what products are available, compare prices and place orders with the farmers/FPOs.

> Features of the platform will be:

-Direct marketplace – FarmLink will connect a farmer to a buyer to reduce any extra middlemen.
-AI demand prediction – Uses past sales, seasonality and market data to predict future demand.
-Smart logistics – Can help arrange transport, group deliveries from nearby and optimise delivery.
-Transparent pricing – Shows price of products plus relevant price of delivery to help farmers and buyers make informed choices.
-Digital payments – Can help make secure digital payments.
-Multilingual – The platform will be in the regional language so farmers in rural areas can still use it.
-Quality – Sellers can provide information on where the product is from, when it was harvested, what grade and quality it is.

> Problem Solving:

The current agricultural supply chain can contain multiple middlemen which can raise the final price of the product whilst decreasing the amount the farmer receives of the consumers money.
Using FarmLink Direct, farmers can directly connect with buyers. Transport costs can be reduced by grouping deliveries and optimising delivery routes. Farmers can use demand predictions to help make more informed decisions.
All prices will be shown clearly so farmers and consumers can see what options are available.

 >Innovation:

This solution is innovative because it contains direct trading, AI demand prediction, logistics and transparent pricing.
This is different to a standard online marketplace as it will focus on helping improve the whole process of farming produce from the farmer to the final buyer.

>Innovative aspects include:
-Match farmers to appropriate buyers using AI.
-Use demand predictions to help plan crops and manage stock.
-Group deliveries to save on transport costs.
-Have digital records of orders, payments and products.
-Have the option to use regional languages to allow farmers with varying levels of digital knowledge to understand.
-Aim to help make trading in agriculture more transparent, efficient and accessible.

>Implementation Strategy:

This project will be created in stages:

Stage 1 – Identifying Requirements

Identify the requirements of the farmers, FPOs, consumers, retailers and transport providers. Understand any issues that are common with pricing, transport, availability and payments.

Stage 2 – Building the platform

Create a sign up and log in feature, farmers can add products, buyers can search for products, orders can be managed and there will be an admin dashboard.

Stage 3 – AI

Create a simple demand prediction model using past sales and seasonality. This will start off being simple but can be improved once reliable data becomes available.

Stage 4 – Logistics

Create a map feature and optimise the delivery routes to help identify the best routes. Orders can be grouped where possible.

Stage 5 – Testing

Test the application with example products, orders, multiple users and example delivery destinations. Check the pricing, orders and predictions are working.

Stage 6 – Trial Run

Run a small trial with selected farmers or FPO. Get feedback, see what results we get and improve the system.

## Feasibility and Viability
> Feasibility:

The idea is feasible as all the required technologies such as web development, database, maps and machine learning are available.
A basic working prototype can be created using open source technologies and example data. This can include farmers signing up, adding products, buyers placing orders, comparing prices and basic delivery coordination.
Features such as AI predictions and advanced logistics can be added gradually as more reliable data becomes available.
Start with a small number of farmers and products before scaling

> Issues & Risks:

-Some issues that could arise are
-Low digital knowledge of some farmers.
-Poor internet connection in rural areas.
-Seasonal changes in supply and demand.
-Cost of transport and lack of available transport.
-Variation in quality and grading.
-Lack of trust between unknown buyers/sellers.
-Data security, payment security and fake listings.
-Not enough past data for accurate AI predictions.

> Solutions:

-Simplify the interface and use regional languages.
-Ensure the platform is mobile friendly with low data.
-Work with FPOs and local agricultural bodies to help get farmers on board.
-Verify sellers, give them ratings and describe the products clearly.
-Have transparent records of orders and secure payments.
-Start with basic predictions and improve as enough data is obtained.
-Group compatible orders and optimise transport.
-Secure personal data with secure access.
-Offer customer service and order resolution.

## Impact and Benefits
> Impact on Target Audience:

Those who will benefit from this are farmers, FPOs, consumers, retailers, restaurants and more bulk buyers.
Farmers will have access to a wider range of buyers and more transparent pricing. Consumers may also benefit from no extra middlemen costs. Bulk buyers will have an easier time finding products and coordinating purchases.
Transport providers may benefit from more organised deliveries.

> Social, Economic & Environmental Impact:

-Economic Impact
-Improved net income for farmers.
-Reduced avoidable costs in supply chain.
-More transparent pricing for consumers.
-Help small farmers and FPOs access the market .
-Social Impact
-More inclusive for small and rural farmers.
-More access to digital services related to agriculture.
-Improved communication between producers and buyers.
-More transparent in agricultural transactions.
-Environmental Impact
-Less unnecessary transport due to optimised routes.
-Better organised deliveries.
-Improved demand planning so less food wastage.

> Expected Outcomes:

-The success of the platform can be measured by:
-Number of farmers and buyers signed up.
-Number of direct transactions made.
-Average net income per unit sold by farmers.
-Price difference between similar prices at farm gate and consumers.
-Average transport costs per delivery.
-On time deliveries.
-Percentage of successful orders.
-Accuracy of predictions and how much product is wasted.
-During the trial run these will be measured. Real improvements will be reported based on the data collected.
